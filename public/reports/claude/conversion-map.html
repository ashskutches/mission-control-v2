Read these before the plan

Four things that change how the numbers read

The blog funnel is already live, and too new to judge

The rebuild in the Rebounder Funnel Rebuild report added product CTAs and a sticky bar to articles. They are on the live site now. Since mid-September, blog add-to-carts went from about zero a day to about two a day, with no orders yet. The 90-day blog figure above is almost entirely from before that work. What the funnel does not have yet is email capture.

Weak desktop conversion is a blog effect

Desktop converts at 0.63% and mobile at 1.12%. Half of all desktop sessions (25,747) are blog readers. Leave the blog out and desktop homepage and product pages convert about as well as mobile. There is no desktop-specific problem to fix.

Facebook shoppers add to cart and then don't buy

Facebook sent 9,793 sessions with 416 add-to-carts, which is a strong 4.2%. Only 29 completed checkout: 7% of carts, against 26% across the site. The likely cause is checkout inside Facebook's in-app browser, where saved addresses and card autofill are missing.

The advertorial's traffic is mostly one odd day

/pages/advertorial-landing shows 1,707 sessions and 1 completed checkout. But 1,275 of those sessions came on 23 September, with no add-to-carts at all. That looks like a traffic spike, not real shoppers. On a normal day the page gets 10 to 60 visits. It still matters, because it is now the page the blog hands readers to.

The plan

Five pages, in the order to build them

Each item lists the evidence, the sections or embeds to build, and the assumed improvement behind its dollar estimate. The order weighs size against effort and Q4 timing.

  1. Cart and checkout: rescue the 60% who stop paying

    /cart · product page buy box

    2,747 sessions reached checkout and 1,091 finished. Checkout itself is Shopify's, but the cart page and buy box before it are ours. Tested with a rebounder in the cart: we found no express checkout buttons on either page. The cart shows no monthly price, and its "Products You Might Like" block rendered empty.

    • Cart trust row: free shipping, 30-day trial, lifetime warranty, and "or $55/mo" in one line under the total.
    • Express checkout buttons (Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay) on the cart and in the product buy box. They skip address entry, which is aimed directly at the Facebook drop-off.
    • Stability bar add-on in the empty "Products You Might Like" slot, sized to the rebounder in the cart. The bar appeared on 474 orders in the window, against 814 for the rebounder, so the demand is proven.
    • Klaviyo check: confirm the abandoned-checkout flow is live and sends within an hour.
    target 40% → 42–44% completed$45k–$90k / yr

  2. Blog articles: capture the readers who aren't ready to buy

    article template · 676 URLs · 46,332 sessions

    Blog readers are mostly just looking for information. The new CTAs cover people ready to buy. Everyone else leaves with nothing. In the old data, the-cons-of-rebounding had 2,089 sessions and 1 add-to-cart, and master-the-plank had 1,173 sessions and none.

    • Inline email capture mid-article, offering the free workout guide plus 10% off. It feeds the existing Klaviyo welcome flow.
    • Exit-intent popup on articles only, with the same offer. Keep it off the product page and checkout.
    • Match the product card to the article's topic, for example the lymphatic post naming the lymphatic benefit. The funnel's context bridge already carries 7 topic variants that this can reuse.
    • Measure the live funnel for 3–4 weeks before changing its CTAs.
    target 0.02% → 0.15–0.3%$45k–$95k / yr

  3. Homepage: show a price and proof near the top

    / · 31,770 sessions · 1.23%

    On a phone the first price appears about 2,270px down. There is no star rating or review count anywhere on the page. A blank 563px section sits right after the NASA quote.

    • Proof line under the hero: "4.7★ · 633 reviews · 30K+ sold" and "From $220 (was $250)".
    • Buy block near the top: a 40" / 48" picker with a real add-to-cart button, instead of "BUY NOW" sending people to another page.
    • Live reviews widget in place of the six text testimonials.
    • Bungee vs springs vs Bellicon comparison table. The argument already exists on the advertorial.
    • Move the email signup up from about 6,200px, and remove or fill the blank section.
    target +10–20% relative$30k–$60k / yr

  4. Rebounder product page: tighten what's already working

    /products/bungee-rebounders-mini-trampoline · 22,230 sessions · 1.54%

    6.6% of sessions add to cart and the bar sells well. Most of the gains here come from consistency and moving things up the page.

    • Star rating under the title. Today the buy box shows "30K+ Rebounders Sold!" and no stars.
    • Name the financing provider. The page just says "Financing Available", with no provider or monthly amount.
    • Move the FAQ up. It sits below a 5,100px benefits section, about 8,900px down the page on mobile.
    • Fix the contradictions listed below.
    target +8–15% relative$20k–$40k / yr

  5. Advertorial: make the page the blog hands off to convert

    /pages/advertorial-landing

    On normal days it gets a few hundred sessions over the window, 9 add-to-carts and 1 completed checkout. Its buttons go through a discount redirect to the product page, which adds a page between reading and buying.

    • Embed the buy block (size, colour, add to cart, discount already applied) on the page itself, below the first two reasons.
    • Line its claims up with the rest of the site (see below).
    • Filter the 23 September spike out before reading any conversion rate for this page.

Timing

Ship before Black Friday

Last November and December brought in $330k together, about double the pace of a normal month. Every point of improvement is worth twice as much in those eight weeks. So the build order is cart and checkout first, then blog email capture, then the homepage proof line and buy block. All three can reach a preview theme within a few weeks.

  • Build on an unpublished copy of the live theme. Today that's "8.4.1 FAQ fix". Review each change through its preview link.
  • Publishing to the live theme is Ash's decision. Nothing in this report has been applied.
  • Measure each change against its own 90-day row in the estimate table.
