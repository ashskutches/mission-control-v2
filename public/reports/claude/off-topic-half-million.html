The corroborating measurement

Our own click-through curve is flat, and that is the tell

Why this matters more than any single page

A healthy site earns roughly 2–3% CTR at positions 7–10. Across 157 of our pages ranking there, the median is 0.60%. That is not a copywriting problem spread evenly across 557 blog posts.

It is the signature of ranking for queries where our result is not the answer. People see us, read the title, recognise it is not what they wanted, and click something else. A flat curve also means ranking higher would not fix it — it would win more impressions at the same dismal rate.

Ruled out Page speed is not the problem

Field data from real Chrome users across the 15 most-seen pages: 15 of 15 pass Core Web Vitals, 0 failing, across 156,158 impressions. Mobile LCP 1.3–1.5s, INP 127–145ms, CLS 0. Do not spend a day here. (A throttled Lighthouse lab score will tell you the opposite; it is simulating a slow phone, not measuring ours.)

One thing we cannot price, and should stop pretending we can

Every figure on this page is clicks, not dollars. GA4 attributes $0 of revenue to every page in the property because purchase events were never configured, so the opportunity ledger prices 0 of 441 pages. Any revenue number attached to this work today would be invented. Fixing GA4 purchase tracking should run alongside Phase 1, or we will finish the work unable to prove it paid.

Recommendation

Build the buying layer the site is missing — from parts it already has

The idea is not new content. It is a hub-and-spoke buying layer, built on the exact pattern the site already runs successfully for workouts, and pointed at the 479 cannibalised commercial queries.

Three pieces of evidence that this fits rather than fights the site:

  • The hub-and-spoke pattern already exists and already works — lrb-workout-hub plus lrb-workout-show drives /blogs/mini-trampoline-workouts, whose 191 spokes earn 0.79% CTR against news’ 0.65%.
  • The comparison machinery exists — funnel-comparison.liquid is in the section library, and /pages/comparisons (“Why We’re Better”) is live with FAQPage schema.
  • The commercial page shape is proven — /pages/advertorial-landing is the highest-CTR non-brand page on the site at 5.78%, position 6.2.

Everything below reuses lrb-shared.css and the existing design system (lrb__section--cream/--paper, lrb__eyebrow, lrb__cta--primary). No new visual language.

PHASE 1

Consolidate the commercial clusters

No design work

Highest leverage and lowest cost. Until 53 URLs stop fighting over “best rebounder trampoline”, nothing else in this plan can rank. Pick the URL already ranking best in each cluster, fold the useful content into it, 301 the rest.

  • Best rebounder / best mini trampoline for adults — 53 URLs collapse to one. Canonical target: the hero product page, which already ranks 11.9 for the head term.
  • Best rebounder for seniors — 37 URLs. /pages/rebounding-for-seniors already ranks best of the set at 10.9; make it canonical and redirect the rest.
  • Best rebounder for lymphatic drainage — 36 URLs, currently spread across positions 17 to 82. One page.
  • Rebounder vs trampoline / vs mini trampoline — 12 URLs into the existing trampoline-vs-rebounder-key-differences.
  • Leave two clusters alone. rebounder-vs-vibration-plate (position 7.4) and bcan-rebounder-reviews (position 4.3) are already winning. Strengthen, do not touch the URL.
  • Run GA4 purchase-event setup in parallel so Phase 1's effect is measurable in revenue and not only in clicks.
PHASE 2

Make /collections/rebounders the destination for buying intent

Reuses funnel-comparison

The page is already optimised correctly and ranks 30.5 with zero clicks, which is a link-equity problem, not a content problem. It also cannot currently compete with a “best rebounder” listicle, because it is a bare product grid.

  • Link to it from every consolidated guide from Phase 1, from the blog hub, and from the main nav. This is the actual fix for position 30.5.
  • Add above-the-grid comparison content using funnel-comparison.liquid — bungee vs spring, sizes, who each model is for — so it answers the comparison query rather than just listing SKUs.
  • Housekeeping already measured: trim the meta description from 191 to ≤160 characters; add alt text to 6 of 11 images.
  • Fix the indexed feed duplicates. Dozens of ?variant=…&utm_source=google&utm_medium=product_sync URLs are indexed as separate organic pages, splitting the hero product's signals. Canonicalise or disallow.
PHASE 3

A Buying Guide hub, mirroring the workout hub

New lrb- section

Gives the consolidated cluster a topical centre and gives internal link equity somewhere to flow from. This is a copy of a pattern that already outperforms on this site, not an experiment.

  • New section lrb-buying-hub modelled directly on lrb-workout-hub, on the same shared stylesheet.
  • Hub page linking the consolidated guides from Phase 1, each with a route into /collections/rebounders or the product page.
  • Build and QA on the Academy working theme 162003058904. Never the live theme 161887944920 — note the IDs in ai_assistants/CLAUDE.md are stale; the store moved to the 8.x line.
PHASE 4

Repoint or retire the off-topic mass

Owner's call

40% of query-visible impressions come from queries working at 0.03–0.28% CTR. Removing them will look like losing traffic and will not lose revenue. This one is genuinely a decision, not a task — so it is last, and it splits in two.

  • Repoint the salvageable ones. Where a page has real volume and an honest bridge to rebounding — “home workout without equipment” (3,744 impressions), “best exercises for seniors to lose belly fat” (1,959) — add a genuine rebounder CTA and an internal link. These are worth keeping.
  • Retire the ones with no bridge. LA Fitness hours, Planet Fitness cancellations and promo codes, wearable tracker round-ups, trampoline backflips, synonyms for “workout”, “fitness definition in biology”. Roughly 9 to 15 pages account for most of the volume. Noindex or delete.
  • Also worth knowing: something other than us has been publishing to the news blog in bulk — machine-written posts in pairs, seconds apart, with repeated “ultimate guide to [keyword]” titles. Identify and stop it before Phase 1, or consolidation will be undone by the next batch.
